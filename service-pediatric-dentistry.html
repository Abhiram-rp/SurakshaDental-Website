Overview

What is pediatric dentistry?

Children's teeth and jaws change quickly. Pediatric dentistry covers routine checks, prevention and treatment of decay, and guidance for parents on brushing, diet and habits.

Visits are paced to suit the child's age and confidence. Early appointments help children become comfortable with the clinic and let problems be found while they are simple to treat.

What we treat

  • First dental visits and routine check-ups
  • Tooth decay and fillings in milk or permanent teeth
  • Fluoride application and sealants
  • Teething and early oral habits
  • Thumb sucking and dummy use
  • Space maintenance after early tooth loss
  • Dental injuries

What to expect

  1. A friendly introduction to the clinic
  2. Examination suited to the child’s age
  3. Preventive care and advice
  4. Treatment if it is needed
  5. Guidance for parents
  6. Recall at an interval suited to the child

FAQs

Pediatric Dentistry questions

When should my child first see a dentist?

Around the first birthday or when the first tooth appears, and no later than the first check-up recommended by your dentist.

Are milk teeth important?

Yes. They help chewing, speech and jaw development, and they guide the permanent teeth into place.

How can I prevent decay?

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, limit sugary snacks and drinks, and keep regular dental appointments.

Is fluoride safe?

Fluoride is used in amounts appropriate for age. Your dentist will advise on toothpaste strength and professional application.

What about thumb sucking?

Most children stop on their own. A prolonged habit can affect the bite and may need guidance.

What if my child is anxious?

Tell us beforehand. Appointments can be paced, and simple behaviour techniques are used to build confidence.

This information is general and does not replace a clinical examination. Please book a consultation for advice about your own situation.
