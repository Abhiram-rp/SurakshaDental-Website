Overview

What is periodontics?

Periodontics focuses on the tissues around the teeth. Gingivitis, or inflammation of the gums, is common and reversible. If it progresses to periodontitis, the bone supporting the teeth can be lost, which may lead to loosening and tooth loss.

Treatment aims to control inflammation and infection, and to help you maintain gum health at home. Care may include professional cleaning, deeper cleaning of root surfaces, and review at intervals suited to your condition.

What we treat

  • Gums that bleed when brushing or flossing
  • Persistent bad breath
  • Red, swollen or tender gums
  • Receding gums or teeth that look longer
  • Loose teeth or changes in the bite
  • Gum pockets found during examination

What to expect

  1. Gum examination and measurement of pockets
  2. Professional cleaning above the gum line
  3. Deeper cleaning of root surfaces if needed
  4. Home care instruction
  5. Maintenance reviews at recommended intervals

At a glance

  • Non-surgical care first
  • Home care guidance
  • Regular maintenance reviews
  • Referral for specialist surgery if needed

FAQs

Periodontics questions

Why do my gums bleed?

Bleeding usually indicates inflammation caused by plaque. It often improves with correct brushing, cleaning between the teeth and professional care.

Does scaling loosen teeth?

No. Scaling removes deposits from the teeth. Teeth may feel different for a short time, but the support around them is not removed.

Can gum disease be cured?

Gingivitis can be reversed. Periodontitis can usually be controlled, but it needs ongoing maintenance to keep it stable.

How often should my gums be checked?

This depends on your condition. Your dentist will recommend an interval, often three to six months for treated periodontal disease.

Does smoking affect gum health?

Yes. Smoking increases the risk of gum disease and can mask bleeding, which makes problems harder to detect.

Is gum disease linked to other conditions?

It has been associated with diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Controlling gum inflammation is part of looking after your overall health.
