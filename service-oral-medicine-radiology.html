Overview

What is oral medicine and radiology?

Oral medicine deals with conditions of the mouth that are not primarily treated by surgery or restorative dentistry. This includes ulcers, white or red patches, persistent pain, dry mouth and disorders of the jaw joint.

Oral radiology covers the imaging used to see teeth, jaws and surrounding structures. Images help confirm a diagnosis, plan treatment and monitor progress. Where a lesion needs further assessment, your dentist will explain the options, including referral or biopsy.

What we treat

  • Mouth ulcers that do not heal
  • White, red or dark patches in the mouth
  • Persistent oral pain or a burning sensation
  • Dry mouth or altered taste
  • Jaw joint pain, clicking or limited opening
  • Swelling or lesions seen on imaging

What to expect

  1. Clinical examination of the mouth and jaws
  2. Imaging if it is clinically indicated
  3. An explanation of the findings
  4. Treatment, monitoring or referral as appropriate
  5. A review appointment where needed

At a glance

  • Clinical examination first
  • X-rays and imaging where indicated
  • Clear explanation of findings
  • Referral when specialist care is needed

FAQs

Oral Medicine and Radiology questions

What is an OPG X-ray?

An orthopantomogram is a single image showing all the teeth, both jaws and the jaw joints. It is used for assessment and treatment planning.

Are dental X-rays safe?

Radiation doses are low and are only used when there is a clinical reason. Precautions are taken, and you should mention pregnancy before any imaging.

When should a mouth ulcer be checked?

If an ulcer lasts more than two to three weeks, or is painful, bleeding or growing, it should be examined.

Will I need a biopsy?

Only if the clinical picture suggests it. A small sample may be taken, or you may be referred to a specialist.

Can jaw joint pain be treated?

Many cases improve with conservative measures such as a soft diet, jaw exercises and habit changes. Persistent cases may need referral.

Will I get a report?

Findings and any images are explained to you. Reports can be shared with your doctor or specialist when needed.
