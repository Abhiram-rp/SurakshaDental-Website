Overview

What are implants and cosmetic dentistry?

A dental implant is a small titanium fixture placed in the jawbone to support a crown, bridge or denture. After healing, the implant fuses with the bone and can function like a natural tooth root.

Cosmetic dentistry covers treatments that change how teeth look, such as professional whitening, composite bonding and veneers. These are elective procedures, so the risks, limits and alternatives are discussed before you decide.

What we treat

  • Single or multiple missing teeth
  • A denture that is loose or uncomfortable
  • Teeth that are discoloured, chipped or unevenly shaped
  • Small gaps or minor alignment concerns
  • A smile that has changed with age or wear

What to expect

  1. Assessment, photographs and imaging
  2. A discussion of suitability, options and cost
  3. Implant placement, if an implant is chosen
  4. A healing period before the final restoration
  5. Fitting of the final crown, bridge or denture
  6. Review and maintenance advice

At a glance

  • Implant planning with imaging
  • Shade and shape discussion
  • Staged treatment where needed
  • Maintenance and review

FAQs

Implants and Cosmetic Dentistry questions

Am I suitable for an implant?

Suitability depends on bone volume, gum health and general health. Smoking and uncontrolled conditions such as diabetes can affect healing.

How long does implant treatment take?

Healing usually takes a few months before the final crown is fitted. The total time varies with the site and whether bone grafting is needed.

Is implant placement painful?

It is carried out under local anaesthesia. Discomfort afterwards is usually manageable with the medication advised by your dentist.

Does whitening damage teeth?

Professional whitening is considered safe when supervised. Temporary sensitivity is common and usually settles.

Are veneers permanent?

Veneers usually involve removing a small amount of enamel, so they are generally not reversible. This is discussed before treatment.

Can I whiten crowns or fillings?

No. Whitening affects natural teeth. Existing restorations may need replacing to match a lighter shade.
