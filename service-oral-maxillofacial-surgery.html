Overview

What is oral and maxillofacial surgery?

Oral and maxillofacial surgery covers procedures that need surgical access, from removing a difficult tooth to treating cysts, infections and jaw problems. Many procedures are carried out under local anaesthesia in the clinic.

Your surgeon will explain why the procedure is recommended, what it involves, and what to expect during recovery. Some cases need imaging or referral to a hospital setting.

What we treat

  • Wisdom teeth that are impacted or causing problems
  • Difficult or broken-down teeth that need extraction
  • Dental infections and abscesses
  • Cysts and some benign growths
  • Jaw fractures or jaw joint problems
  • Soft-tissue procedures such as frenectomy

What to expect

  1. Assessment and imaging
  2. An explanation of the procedure and your consent
  3. The procedure under local anaesthesia
  4. Written aftercare instructions
  5. A review appointment after surgery

At a glance

  • Local anaesthesia for most procedures
  • Imaging before complex surgery
  • Written aftercare instructions

FAQs

Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery questions

Do all wisdom teeth need removing?

No. Only those causing problems, or with a high risk of doing so, need removal. Teeth without symptoms may simply be monitored.

Will I be awake during surgery?

Most procedures use local anaesthesia, so you are awake but the area is numb. Sedation or general anaesthesia may be arranged for selected cases.

How long is recovery?

Swelling and discomfort usually peak in the first two to three days and settle over a week or two, depending on the procedure.

Can I smoke after surgery?

Avoid smoking for at least a few days. It increases the risk of infection and slows healing.

What is a dry socket?

It is a painful condition that occurs when the blood clot is lost after an extraction. Contact the clinic if pain increases after the first few days.

Will I need stitches?

Some procedures need stitches. They may dissolve on their own or be removed at a review appointment.

This information is general and does not replace a clinical examination. Please book a consultation for advice about your own situation.
